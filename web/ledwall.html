The LED wall
that wasn't

We built a five-panel LED wall to show the tumor in glowing 3D slices. Then one of the power adapters started smoking and sparking, even though the wiring checked out. We unplugged it for good. Here's what it would have looked like, and why it's not part of the demo.

// 01 · what it would have shown

A tumor in five slices

Flat panels can't show a sphere, so each panel was going to be one slice through the tumor, front to back: small, big, biggest, big, small. The preview below is the real code that would have driven the wall (web/ledframe.js), running on a live simulation in your browser.

how slices map to panels

Each LED averaged a small patch of grid cells, so colors matched the 3D view: blue rim, dark resting core, and resistant clones in their pill bottle's color.

// 02 · how it was wired

The planned build

The browser did all the drawing and streamed finished frames over USB. The Arduino just passed bytes to the panels. Each panel got its own 5 V supply through a barrel-jack-to-screw-terminal adapter, and that adapter is where things went wrong.

// 03 · why we didn't use it

Safety beat the light show

Built

A test sketch lit a single panel from the Uno. The browser-side frame code and preview were done and tested.

Debugged

After swapping between an Uno and a Mega, the panels stopped responding, on more than one panel.

Smoke

One of the barrel-jack adapters on a power cable started smoking and sparking.

Checked

We went back over the wiring and it was correct, which made it more worrying, not less.

Unplugged

No replacement on hand, and we wouldn't trust one from the same batch. The wall is retired.

Lots of current

1,280 LEDs at up to 60 mA each is about 77 A at full white. Even capped low, that's a lot running through small screw terminals and cheap adapters, all of it heat.

Correct isn't always safe

Correct wiring doesn't protect you from a bad part. A loose terminal, an underrated adapter, or a manufacturing defect can still get hot enough to smoke.

A room full of people

This was going on a table at a hackathon with people leaning over it. A part that's already sparked once doesn't belong there.

// 04 · what replaced it

The colors moved to the screen

Everything the wall would have shown is in the 3D simulator, and the pill bottles still pick the treatment. Each bottle's color is the resistance color, so you still watch your treatment's color take over when the tumor learns to beat it.

key 1 · red bottle

Doxorubicin

Resisted by: RCH or the drug pump (EFX)

Why red: doxorubicin really is red, nicknamed the "red devil".

key 2 · black bottle

Photofrin (porfimer)

Resisted by: RCH

Why black: patients have to stay out of bright light afterward.

key 3 · orange bottle

Goserelin

Resisted by: target escape (RTA)

Why orange: hormone therapy.

key 4 · pink bottle

Enhertu

Resisted by: RTA or the drug pump (EFX)

Why pink: it's for HER2 breast cancer (pink ribbon).

key 5 · yellow bottle

Radiation

Resisted by: better DNA repair (RAD)

Why yellow: the radiation warning sign.

key 6 · green bottle

Pembrolizumab

Resisted by: immune escape (IMM)

Why green: a green light for T cells.

key 7 · grey bottle

Carboplatin

Resisted by: RCH

Why grey: platinum is a grey metal.

key 8 · white bottle

Paclitaxel

Resisted by: RCH or the drug pump (EFX)

Why white: it comes from the pale bark of the yew tree.
